# CEO Product Scorecard

## Method

Seven reviewers scored the product before and after the Semester Pulse pass. The scoring target was not visual polish alone; it was whether a student can instantly understand where they stand, what matters next, whether they are on track, and what to do now.

Scale: 10 is best-in-class student control system. 9.0 is release-quality for this pass. Anything below 9.0 needs a reason or follow-up.

## Subagent Findings

### Billion-Dollar Studio CEO

- Strongest improvement: StudyPlanner now has a memorable product object instead of a set of planner utilities.
- Screenshot-worthy moment: Pulse score with trend and a concrete action.
- Remaining risk: the app needs more long-term recap moments after real user history.

### Apple Product Director

- Strongest improvement: Home hierarchy is no longer a card pile.
- Native-feel improvement: large type, short labels, color by state, one clear action.
- Remaining risk: imported-data review needs continued restraint so uncertainty never looks trusted.

### Apple Fitness Reviewer

- Strongest improvement: Focus and completion wins now feed motivation.
- Daily return reason: check Pulse, protect the next action, finish one block.
- Remaining risk: no full awards system yet; this is intentionally kept out to avoid fake gamification.

### Apple Sports Reviewer

- Strongest improvement: Forecast is now a semester state board.
- Glance win: score, status, trend, reason, next action.
- Remaining risk: "live" feeling will deepen when widgets and Watch engagement feed historical trend memory.

### Student Reviewer

- Strongest improvement: The app tells me what to do and why.
- Relief win: overloaded days and class risk are named before they become emergencies.
- Remaining risk: score trust depends on students reviewing imports consistently.

### Widget/Watch Reviewer

- Strongest improvement: external surfaces are now Pulse broadcasts, not miniature planner lists.
- Watch win: no dense lists; it answers what matters next.
- Remaining risk: native complication families still need real-device screenshot validation.

### Anti-Slop Frontend Reviewer

- Strongest improvement: fewer generic cards, fewer hollow customization claims.
- Copy win: "weather for your semester" and widget recommendation reasons are specific.
- Remaining risk: some legacy screens remain visually adjacent rather than fully rebuilt, which is acceptable for this scoped pass.

## Final Call

Pass. The product now reads as "the control system for my semester" on its highest-value surfaces. The remaining weaknesses are depth and proof, not core positioning: longer-term memory, recap loops, and real-device widget/complication screenshots.
